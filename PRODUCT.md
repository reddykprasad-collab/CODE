# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Migraine patients on one of two journeys:

**Awareness path**: Undertreated or newly exploring options. May not know preventive therapy exists or that they qualify. Frustrated by a condition they've learned to live around. Looking for language and data to have a better conversation with their doctor.

**Adherence path**: Currently on a preventive treatment. Tracking doses, logging symptoms, building evidence that treatment is or isn't working. Motivated but uncertain. Needs to see progress to stay consistent.

Both groups use the app between and around medical appointments, not during them. They are often in pain or recovering when they pick it up.

## Product Purpose

Help migraine patients understand their burden, track their treatment, and walk into their next HCP appointment with real data and the right words. Disease-state only. No brand names, no dosing advice, no diagnosis. The companion does the background work (logging, trend analysis, summary generation) so the patient can focus on the conversation that matters.

Success: a patient who previously said "I get a lot of headaches" now says "I've had 11 migraine days in the last 30 days, my average severity is 7.4, and I've missed work 4 times" — and has a summary in their pocket to prove it.

## Brand Personality

Warm, grounded, honest. Empathetic first, clinical second. Feels like a knowledgeable friend who happens to understand migraine medicine, not a doctor or a wellness coach.

## Anti-references

**Headspace / meditation / wellness apps**: No gradient blobs, no motivational copy, no "you've got this" energy, no abstract organic shapes. This is not a spa. It is not telling the user to breathe. The condition is serious and the app treats it that way.

**Consumer health gamification**: No streak fire emojis, no confetti, no badges. Dose confirmation is functional, not celebratory. A person who missed their medication is not being punished; a person who took it is not being rewarded with sparkles.

**Pharma patient portals**: No cold white clinical forms, no navy-and-teal health system color schemes, no jargon-heavy interface that reads like a prescription insert.

## Design Principles

**Dignified, not cheerful.** The user is managing a painful, chronic condition. The interface should be warm without being upbeat. Acknowledge the difficulty without wallowing in it.

**Data earns trust.** Credibility comes from showing the user real numbers — their numbers — clearly and without noise. Every visualization, every summary, every trend should make the patient's lived experience legible to themselves and their doctor.

**Say the hard thing plainly.** If the signal is "you may be undertreated," say it. If the pattern is worsening, show it. Soften the tone, not the content.

**Quiet at rest, purposeful in action.** The interface should feel calm when browsing. When something needs attention — a dose to confirm, a result to review, a summary to share — it should be decisively, unmistakably clear.

**Function earns the surface.** Every visual element must serve the patient's goal. Decoration that doesn't work for the user is clutter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. The app is used by people in pain, sometimes with photosensitivity or visual sensitivity during a migraine event. High contrast text, generous tap targets, and no reliance on color alone for meaning. Reduced motion accommodated.
